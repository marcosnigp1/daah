For Assignment 1, we used distant-reading methods to explore how science fiction writing changed before, during, and after World War II. Our main question was whether the World War II period was associated with a stronger interest in science-fiction themes, especially space and war.

## What we investigated

We divided the corpus into three periods:

- Before World War II
- During World War II (1939–1945)
- After World War II

We then compared the periods using frequent-word counts, TF-IDF distinctive vocabulary, thematic tags, and book-length data.

## What we found

The results suggest that space-related language became more visible during and after World War II. Terms such as “spacemen,” “spaceships,” “spacecraft,” “starship,” and “spaceport” appeared among the distinctive words for these periods.

The thematic-tag comparison showed a similar pattern. Space travel was much more common during World War II than before it, while war-related themes appeared throughout the corpus. These patterns may relate to technological developments, the increased visibility of aviation, and the cultural effects of the war, although the data cannot establish a single cause.

We also investigated why “Tarzan” appeared so frequently in the results. Comparing book length and publication year helped us identify outliers and showed why individual books can strongly influence corpus-level patterns.

## Methods

We used the following methods:

- Frequent words per 1,000 words
- TF-IDF comparison of distinctive vocabulary
- Thematic-tag frequencies
- Average book-length comparisons
- Scatter plots for investigating outliers
